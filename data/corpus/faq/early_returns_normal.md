# Is it normal for a SIP to show flat or negative returns early on?

Because a SIP buys fund units at a different NAV each month, it naturally buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high — this is called rupee cost averaging. As a result, the overall return of a SIP in its first several months can look flat or even negative, even when the underlying investment is behaving exactly as designed. A SIP typically takes longer than a lump-sum investment to reflect the broader market trend, since each instalment has its own, more recent starting point.
